**The dot is a real element, and the board's is not.** The pill's `::before` computes `content: "none"`; the
product renders `<span class="tick-dot tick-dot-verified">` inside `[data-tick][data-tick-tone]`. That is why
an instrument may assert WHICH TONE a tick wears and may not assert that it is olive: the tone is a DOM
attribute and the colour is CSS. The suite asserts the first and never the second.

### The false alarm, recorded because it is the round's sharpest lesson

A probe over `document.body.textContent` reported the legal disclaimer **PRESENT** on the gated view. It was
not. The sole occurrence sat inside a `<script>` — Next.js's RSC payload carrying the message catalogue, at
rect `[0,0]`, visible `false`. Re-measured over a body clone with `script`, `style`, `template` and
`noscript` removed: **none shown**.

**The LANDED instrument was right and the probe was wrong.** `noDisclaimerOffTheThesis` reads through
`shownText()` and had been green throughout. This is the third instance in one round of **an instrument's
OUTPUT being mistaken for its MEASUREMENT** — and the first two were the same shape: a `grep -c` over a
profiler's whole output, and a citation read from a search window rather than its enclosing unit. A probe
written in a hurry to check a landed instrument is a second implementation of that instrument, with none of
its care.

## 6. (f) THE PARSER THAT NARROWED — a defect nothing could have failed on

`lib/researchBody.ts`' `mention()` kept four fields of a citation — `kind`, `name`, `pin`, `argued` — and
dropped **`record`, `content`, `verified`, `flag` and `overObjection`**. The backend had served all nine
since R70 (`getThesisContext.ts` :71's `ResolvedMention`), and thesis A4 :1476 had ruled since 2026-09-21
that `V.mentions` is the ONE citation shape, "public and gated".

**Nothing failed.** The parse succeeded, the body was valid, `tsc` was clean, the page rendered, and the
fields were simply gone. No case could have caught it, because no surface drew them yet: the centre is what
made them load-bearing, and the centre did not exist.

**The shape, which is the transferable part: a parser that NARROWS looks identical to a parser that
VALIDATES.** Both accept a body and return a smaller type; both are green; only one is a reader of the
contract. The appendices already name this failure one level up — *an envelope read off an implementation
states what is SERVED, never what is OWED* — and `ui §6.1 :248` is its precedent. This is the same error in
the READER rather than in the envelope, and it is harder to see, because an envelope can be compared to a
body while a parser is usually compared only to itself.

**The fix was to CALL rather than to widen:** `lib/thesisBody.ts`' `citation()` — the public page's parser,
written to the same clause — was exported, and the gated reader calls it. One citation shape, one parser.
